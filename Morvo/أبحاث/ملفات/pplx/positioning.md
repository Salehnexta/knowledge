**Morvo should position itself as a Saudi market decision brief for independent store owners—not a “free store audit,” an ad-spy database, or an AI analytics platform.** The distinctive promise is: *show me what changed in my category, what competitors are visibly doing, and which one decision I should test next—with the evidence attached.* That is a narrower and more credible offer than promising to find “winning ads” or diagnose a store from its URL alone. Saudi agencies already offer free diagnoses, and Salla offers a free, no-registration store analyzer that includes search and AI-search visibility; neither “free analysis” nor GEO by itself is defensible positioning.[web:94][web:100]

### Saudi positioning space

These are **relative openings, not claims that nobody offers any component**. In particular, Salla already offers free SEO/GEO store analysis to merchants on any platform, without registration; Saudi agencies advertise free diagnosis and data-led recommendations.[web:100][web:94][web:97]

| Positioning angle | What Morvo could actually show | Why it is more distinctive than a free audit or ChatGPT | Boundary to state plainly |
|---|---|---|---|
| **“What changed in your Saudi category this week?”** | A dated category brief: newly observed competitor ads and offers, price changes, Saudi search demand, and the latest relevant SAMA POS and GASTAT CPI releases. | Changes the unit of value from a **static store inspection** to a repeatable market watch. SAMA publishes weekly spending by activity and city; GASTAT’s CPI measures changes in a consumer basket.[web:110][web:107] | POS is economy-wide payment activity, **not the merchant’s online sales**; CPI is **not a competitor’s SKU price**.[web:110][web:107] |
| **“Which offer should I test next—and why?”** | One prioritized test, such as an offer, price presentation, product-page claim or ad angle, with competitor examples, dated observations and an explicitly stated assumption. | Supplies a small merchant with a **decision and its evidence**, rather than an agency’s broad diagnosis or a chatbot’s plausible suggestions. | **“See the evidence before you pay for the conclusion.”** | A free preview containing named competitors, a few dated observations, source links and freshness dates; the **199 SAR** report unlocks the comparison, prioritization and test plan. | Creates a concrete reason to pay after a useful preview. | **“Arabic-first competitive evidence, across store platforms.”** | Saudi-relevant Arabic/English queries, locally comparable prices and offers, Arabic creative angles, and a readable brief usable by a Salla, Zid or other-platform owner. | The differentiation is the **combination and interpretation for a Saudi merchant**, not merely Arabic UI or platform independence—Salla’s free tools are themselves open to any merchant.[web:100] | Only claim category or competitor coverage where Morvo can demonstrate it. |

**Recommended homepage wording**

> **اعرف وش تغيّر في سوقك السعودي — ووش تختبر بعدها.**  
> راقب عروض وأسعار وإعلانات منافسيك، وشوف إشارات الطلب والإنفاق الرسمية في مكان واحد. خذ قرارًا واحدًا واضحًا للتجربة، مع روابط الأدلة وتاريخ كل معلومة. **معاينة مجانية؛ التقرير الكامل 199 ر.س، دفعة واحدة.**

In English: **“Know what changed in your Saudi market—and what to test next.”** Put an actual sample decision card directly beneath that headline: *observation → source and date → interpretation → recommended test → KPI and review date*. This is the part neither a generic prompt nor an agency’s promise of a free audit reliably supplies.

The credibility rule is as important as the copy. For ordinary commercial Meta ads, the public library does **not** disclose competitors’ spend, conversions or ROAS; ad longevity is a signal to investigate, not proof an ad is profitable.[web:104] Mark each finding **observed**, **estimated**, or **hypothesis**. That turns Morvo’s real advantage—reproducible, Saudi-specific evidence translated into a modest next decision—into something a skeptical merchant can inspect before paying.
