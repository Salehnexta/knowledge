# Morvo — Project Goals

## Vision

Morvo, by Vionx, is a Saudi growth intelligence platform for online stores. It reads public market data about a store and its competitors and turns it into clear business decisions: where the store is losing, who is ahead of it, and what to do next.

We think like a business developer sitting with the merchant, not an analyst listing numbers. Every number must answer: *"What does this mean for my revenue, and what should I do?"*

## The problem

Online store owners make expensive decisions blind:

- They spend on ads without knowing where the money goes.
- Competitors sell more, and they don't know what those competitors are doing.
- Influencer deals are a gamble, with no way to tell what works.
- They don't know whether their prices are right compared with the market.
- Customers search for their products and find someone else in Google, and AI assistants recommend competitors.
- Global tools are complex, English-only and expensive, and agency reports are hard to act on.

## Who we serve

| Customer | What they get |
|---|---|
| **Store owners without marketing expertise** | Three ranked priorities with one clear action each, in plain Arabic, plus a one-minute way to verify every claim themselves. |
| **Marketers, store managers and agencies** | Hours of manual research saved, evidence with source and date for every number, ready-made competitor comparisons, and a 30-day plan to build on. |

## Goals

### 1. Deliver advice that produces measurable return
- Every insight links to revenue impact and a concrete next step.
- Opportunities are ranked by expected impact, not by data source.
- We state plainly what cannot be known from public data.

### 2. Evidence over opinion
- Every number shows its source, observation date and type: **measured**, **estimated** or **calculated**.
- Estimates are shown as ranges. Nothing is invented.
- A human reviews every paid analysis before delivery.

### 3. Built for the Saudi market
- Arabic-first, Saudi search behaviour, Saudi AI-assistant answers, and the ad and social platforms that matter locally.
- Compliance with the Saudi Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL): public data only, no names of private individuals, aggregate-only benchmarks.

### 4. Build a proprietary market dataset
- Every analysis stores structured, dated snapshots: competitor ads (start date, duration, creative type, message), prices, influencer activity, search demand and AI-assistant mentions.
- Over time this becomes sector benchmarks no one else has, such as which ad formats last longest, how influencer tiers perform and where price bands sit.
- Benchmarks are published only as aggregates, with minimum group sizes, and never expose an individual store.

### 5. Move from one-off reports to an ongoing growth partner
Start with a free diagnosis, then a paid analysis, and grow into a living dashboard with a monthly subscription.

## Principles

- **Customer value first:** we describe benefits to the merchant, not our technology.
- **Honesty:** no guaranteed results, no unverifiable "best" claims, no invented statistics or testimonials.
- **Data separation:** a client's private data is never shown to another client and never shapes another client's advice. Competitor views use public data only. Client identities stay confidential.
- **Cost discipline:** every data call has a spending cap, costs are measured per report, and no live paid run happens without approval.
- **Standalone runtime:** the app runs independently, with all AI models through one gateway.

## Success measures

- Free-diagnosis to paid-analysis conversion rate.
- Share of insights the merchant acts on, measured by survey now and connected data later.
- Subscription retention (Phase 2 onward).
- Measured return for connected clients (Phase 3 onward).
- Cost per report within budget: about $0.03 for the free diagnosis and under $1 for the full analysis (current measurements).
- Size and coverage of the proprietary sector dataset.
